1. Product Overview   UPDATED
A hostel mess serves approximately 300 students three times a day. Feedback currently arrives through shouting, scattered messages, and long comments that a mess manager does not have enough time to read and organize. The proposed system provides a fast student feedback experience and converts the collected data into concise, actionable insights for the mess manager.
Product Goal
Create a simple web platform that captures structured, anonymous and verified meal feedback, analyzes optional comments with AI, shows trends, generates weekly reports, alerts the manager to important issues, and allows students to vote on upcoming meals from provider-approved options.
2. Problem Statement   UPDATED
A hostel mess serves around 300 students three times a day. Feedback comes as shouting, scattered messages and long comments that the manager has no time to read.
Critical Constraints
A student should be able to leave feedback in under 10 seconds (timer starts when the rating page opens).
The manager should be able to understand the day's feedback in about 2 minutes.
The interface should minimize typing and unnecessary steps.
The system should prevent repeated ratings for the same student and meal.
AI should summarize and organize feedback rather than create unnecessary complexity.
Anonymity: students must be able to give honest feedback without the cook, manager or provider being able to identify them.
Validity: only students who were actually at the meal can rate it, and only during its rating window.
Provider trust: the provider should see the tool as a fair performance record, not a complaint machine.
3. Product Objectives   UPDATED
Make meal feedback extremely fast and simple.
Convert unstructured comments into clear themes such as taste, quantity, hygiene and delays.
Give the manager a compact daily view of what needs attention.
Show rating trends by meal and by day.
Generate a weekly report containing recurring issues and suggested actions.
Allow students to participate in upcoming menu planning through controlled polls.
Create a feedback loop so students can see that recurring issues are being addressed.
NEW: Protect student identity so feedback is honest.
NEW: Keep the data trustworthy by accepting feedback only from real diners during the meal window.
NEW: Give the provider clear benefits and give the warden / mess committee transparent read-only oversight.

7. Identity, Anonymity & Trust   NEW
7.1 Principle
Duplicates must be prevented, so the system must know a rating came from a verified student. But students will only be honest if they are sure the cook, manager and provider cannot tell who complained. The solution is a pseudonymous ID: the system knows the feedback comes from one verified student, but the people running the mess do not know which one.
7.2 How It Works
One-time verification: the student verifies with a hostel-issued identifier (for example college email or roll number) plus an OTP. The exact method is an open decision (Section 23).
Pseudonymous ID: after verification the system creates a random anonymous ID for the student. All ratings, chips, comments and votes are stored against this ID, never against the name or room.
Separate storage: the mapping between real identity and anonymous ID is stored separately with restricted, logged access.
Stay signed in: the session persists on the student's phone so the 10-second constraint is not broken by repeated logins.

7.4 Additional Safeguards
Small-group suppression: theme breakdowns and filters are hidden or merged when fewer than a minimum number of responses (default 5, configurable), so a single student cannot be singled out.
Comment redaction: names, room numbers and phone numbers are removed from comments before the manager sees them.
Coarse timestamps: comments show meal and date only, not the exact minute they were posted.
Abuse handling: the manager can flag an abusive or threatening comment. The Platform Admin reviews it and can warn or suspend the anonymous ID without revealing the student to the provider.
Honest disclosure: at first login students see a plain-language note: feedback is anonymous to the mess staff and provider, the hostel's Platform Admin can link an ID to a person only for abuse review, and what data is stored and for how long.
8. Feedback Validity: QR and Rating Window   NEW   v3 UPDATED
8.1 Goal
Make sure ratings come from students who were actually at that meal, and stop casual fake ratings, revenge ratings and coordinated bombing.
8.2 Meal Sessions and Rating Window
For every meal in the schedule, the system creates a Meal Session with a start time, an end time and a rating window.
Default rating window: from meal start until 60 minutes after meal end (configurable per meal).
Ratings submitted outside the window are rejected with a clear message.
No session is created for closed days or meals marked “mess closed”.
8.3 Meal QR
Each Meal Session has its own QR code containing a token unique to that meal.
The QR is displayed at the mess (printed daily sheet or a screen at the counter). Delivery method is an open decision (Section 23).
Scanning the QR opens the rating page for that meal and confirms the student was present.
The token expires when the rating window closes, so a photo of yesterday's QR is useless.
Optional hardening: a short rotating code shown at the counter for extra checking, if abuse is observed.
8.4 Abuse Controls
One rating per anonymous ID per meal/date; duplicate attempts are rejected.
Rate limits per device and connection.
Automatic flags for suspicious patterns: a burst of very low ratings in a short time, many identical comments, or many submissions from one device.
Flagged ratings are shown separately and excluded from averages until reviewed (configurable). Nothing is silently deleted.
Poll votes can only be cast by verified, active students, one per poll.
8.5 Impact on the 10-Second Target
The physical QR scan takes a couple of seconds and is treated as arriving at the rating page. The 10-second target is measured from the moment the rating page opens to the confirmation screen.
8.6 QR Scan Log & Rating-Window Override   v3 NEW
Because the provider controls the mess counter, the provider must not be able to suppress feedback simply by not displaying the QR.
Scan log: the system records the first scan time and the total number of scans for each meal session (counts only, no student identity).
No-scan alert: if a meal has no scans within a configurable time after it starts (default 30 minutes), the manager and the warden / committee are alerted.
Window override: a designated Warden / Committee member can open or extend the rating window for a meal, or issue a temporary meal code, with a mandatory reason. The maximum extension is configurable.
Transparency: every override is logged (who, when, why) and shown to the manager and the Platform Admin.
Limited scope: the override only affects when and how students can rate. It cannot edit, delete or view any rating or student identity.
9. Reason Chips   NEW
9.1 Flow
Student taps a star rating (1 to 5). Immediately below, a row of large tappable chips appears. The student can tap up to three chips or skip. The comment box stays optional. Submitting never requires a chip.
9.2 Chip Design
Default chips: Taste, Quantity, Hygiene, Food quality, Temperature, Delay / Service. These match the theme list already used in the PRD.
Adapts to the rating: 1–3 stars shows “What went wrong?”. 4–5 stars shows “What was good?”. The same chips are used, and the star value gives the direction (positive or negative).
Configurable: the chip list can be changed by admin/provider without a code change.
Mobile friendly: large tap targets, icon plus label, and optional Hindi labels.
9.3 Why It Matters
The manager gets structured reasons from day one, for example “Low-rated lunch: Taste 18, Temperature 11”.
Reliability does not depend on AI or on language understanding.
AI in Phase 2 only has to handle free-text comments and merge them with chip data.
If AI is down, the dashboard still shows reasons.
10. Provider Value & Oversight   NEW   v3 UPDATED
10.1 Why the Provider Benefits
Less shouting: complaints arrive in one organized place instead of at the counter.
Two-minute daily view: top reasons and needs-attention items, so problems are caught before they escalate.
Proof of good work: positive themes, best-rated meals and improving trends are reported with the same prominence as complaints.
Credit for fixes: action tracking shows the warden and students what was fixed and when.
Fair data: fake and flagged ratings are filtered, so one angry burst does not damage the provider's record.
Context notes: the provider can attach a note to a meal (for example “gas supply delayed”) that appears alongside the rating for the manager and the read-only viewers.
10.2 Warden / Mess Committee (Read-only)
Can view: response counts, average ratings, meal-wise and day-wise trends, top themes, issue status and weekly reports, plus the provider's action notes.
Cannot: edit menus, change issue status, create polls, or see any student identity.
Individual comments are hidden by default. Showing redacted comments to this role is an open decision (Section 23).
Sees the same numbers as the provider, so there are no separate versions of the truth.
v3  The only action this role can take is the rating-window / QR override in Section 8.6. All other data stays read-only.
10.3 Design Rules Against the “Complaint Machine” Perception
Show positive and negative themes with equal weight.
Show the number of responses next to every average.
Weekly report opens with what went well before recurring issues.
Suggestions are advisory and never change operations automatically.
11. Weekly Menu Upload & Meal Schedule   NEW
11.1 Meal Schedule (one-time setup)
Manager/admin sets meal types (breakfast, lunch, dinner) with start and end times.
Sets the rating-window buffer and marks closed days.
Meal timings drive “current meal” detection and the rating window.
11.2 Weekly Menu
Entry: manager creates a menu by day and meal, each with a list of dishes.
Save time: “copy last week” as a starting point; optional spreadsheet (CSV/Excel) template upload.
Draft then publish: menu is saved as a draft and goes live to students when published.
Reminders: the manager is reminded a couple of days before the week ends if next week's menu is not published.
Edits after publishing: allowed, logged, and the affected meal shows “Menu changed”.
Special flags: special/festival meal, late meal, mess closed.
Student view: today's dishes on the rating page and an upcoming-week menu view.
11.3 Fallback
If no menu is uploaded for a meal, students can still rate it by meal type (for example “Lunch”), so feedback is never blocked. The manager dashboard shows a “Menu not uploaded” flag.
11.4 Link to Menu Voting
The provider may use poll results when filling a future menu slot. The menu decision stays with the provider.

13. AI Requirements   UPDATED
Language understanding: process short comments written in different languages, including mixed-language text (for example Hindi written in English letters). The list of supported languages must be stated and tested on real comments.
Theme extraction: identify topics such as taste, quantity, hygiene, temperature, quality and delays; combine with reason-chip data.
Sentiment/feedback direction: distinguish positive, neutral and negative feedback where useful.
Aggregation: combine many similar comments into a concise theme-level insight.
Recurrence detection: identify issues appearing repeatedly over several meals/days.
Summary generation: produce a short manager-readable daily and weekly summary, always alongside counts so the manager can check it.
Suggested actions: advisory only; they must not automatically change the menu or operations.
NEW: Redact names, room numbers and phone numbers before comments are shown or summarized.
NEW: The AI must not expose or infer private student information in any manager or warden summary.

21. Example End-to-End Scenario   UPDATED
On Sunday the manager copies last week's menu, edits two dishes and publishes it. At lunch, a student scans the QR at the counter. The page shows lunch and today's dishes. The student taps 2 stars, taps “Taste” and “Temperature”, and submits in a few seconds. Another student adds a comment in their own language. The system stores one valid rating per anonymous ID for that meal.
The manager's dashboard shows total responses, average rating, and top reasons, with comments summarized by AI and personal details removed. The manager never sees who rated. If a problem becomes unusually frequent, the dashboard highlights it. The warden opens the read-only view and sees the same numbers and the provider's action notes. At the end of the week the system generates a report showing recurring issues and suggested actions.
For an upcoming meal, the provider creates a poll with a few eligible options. Students vote once. The final result shows the student preference, while the provider considers operational constraints such as budget, ingredients, nutrition and kitchen capacity before finalizing the menu.
22. Product USP   UPDATED
“Students tell the mess what happened today and what they would like to eat next, anonymously and only if they were really there, while AI turns hundreds of responses into a concise, actionable view for the manager.”
The product is differentiated by three things: extremely fast student feedback, extremely low reading effort for the manager, and a trust design that protects students and treats the provider fairly.

25. Edge Cases & Dispute Process   v3 NEW
25.1 No Smartphone or Shared Phones
During onboarding, find out how many students have no smartphone before choosing a solution.
Options: a shared tablet at the counter where a student verifies with a short code and rates (slower), or an assisted rating by a volunteer. Which one to use is an open decision.
On shared phones, each student uses their own verified account and the app always shows which account is signed in, with an easy sign-out.
25.2 Guests and Non-residents
Guests cannot rate because they have no verified ID. Guest counts and guest passes are out of scope.
25.3 Missed or Late Windows
The default rating window ends 60 minutes after the meal ends. After that, rating is closed with a clear message. If there is a genuine reason to reopen, the Warden / Committee can use the override (Section 8.6).
25.4 Changing or Losing a Phone
The student verifies again on the new device and receives the same anonymous ID. This prevents duplicate identities and duplicate ratings.
25.5 Provider Disputes
The manager can raise a dispute on a meal's ratings within 48 hours (configurable), for example for suspected fake or coordinated ratings, or an operational reason such as a supply failure.
The Platform Admin and the warden review the dispute together with the abuse flags.
Possible outcomes: keep as is, exclude the flagged ratings from averages, or attach a context note.
The provider can never delete ratings. Every dispute and outcome is logged and visible to the warden.

27. UI / UX DESIGN DIRECTION — v3.1
Design Goal — Apni Rasoi should look like a clean, trustworthy and modern food-management/feedback platform. The interface must NOT feel like a gaming website, entertainment app or overly dark dashboard.
27.1 Overall Visual Style
• Use a predominantly white and light interface with a soft, clean visual hierarchy.
• The design should feel professional, friendly, simple and suitable for students, mess managers and wardens.
• Avoid heavy dark themes, neon colors, glowing effects, gaming-style cards, excessive gradients and futuristic HUD-style elements.
• Use generous white space, clear sections, restrained rounded cards, subtle borders and light shadows.
• The visual language should communicate food, cleanliness, trust, simplicity and community rather than gaming or entertainment.
27.2 Color Direction
• Primary background: white or very light neutral/off-white.
• Secondary surfaces: very light gray or warm off-white for cards, sections and dashboard panels.
• Primary accent: a fresh food-related color such as green, used in a restrained way.
• Optional secondary accent: a warm food-related tone such as orange/yellow for highlights, polls or positive actions.
• Text should use dark charcoal for readability rather than making the entire interface black.
• Success, warning and error colors should be used only for status communication and remain visually balanced.
• Dark colors may be used for small text, navigation or contrast, but the overall UI must remain light.
27.3 Visual Elements to Avoid
• No full dark-mode-first design.
• No black/purple neon gaming aesthetic.
• No excessive glow, glassmorphism or cyberpunk effects.
• No animated backgrounds that distract from the feedback flow.
• No oversized decorative graphics that make the student rating process slower.
• No complex dashboard layouts that make the manager's two-minute review difficult.
27.4 Student UI
• The student experience is mobile-first and extremely simple.
• The current meal and today's dishes should be visible immediately.
• Use large, easy-to-tap star rating controls and reason chips.
• Keep the rating action visually dominant while keeping optional comments secondary.
• Use simple food-related icons/illustrations where useful, but never let decoration interfere with the under-10-second feedback target.
• Confirmation screens should be clear and reassuring, using concise messages.
• The upcoming menu, active poll and 'You Said → We Did' feed should use clean cards with clear status labels.
27.5 Manager Dashboard UI
• Use a light dashboard layout with a clear information hierarchy.
• The first screen should prioritize today's response count, average rating, response rate and important issues.
• Use simple charts with restrained colors and sufficient white space.
• AI summaries should appear in clean summary cards rather than large blocks of raw text.
• Needs Attention, positive themes and recurring issues should be visually distinguishable without an aggressive or alarm-heavy design.
• Menu management, polls, reports and settings should use a consistent sidebar/top-navigation structure.
• The dashboard must support the existing requirement that a manager can understand the day's feedback in approximately two minutes.
27.6 Warden / Mess Committee UI
• Use the same light visual system as the manager dashboard.
• Clearly indicate that the interface is read-only except for the permitted rating-window/QR override.
• Use clean aggregated charts, response rates, issue status and weekly reports.
• Do not expose student identity information.
27.7 Admin UI
• Use a professional light administrative interface.
• Identity-management, abuse review and access logs should be presented as structured tables/cards.
• Security-sensitive actions should have clear confirmation and status feedback.
• Avoid unnecessary visual decoration in admin workflows.
27.8 Typography & Components
• Use a modern, highly readable sans-serif font.
• Headings should have clear hierarchy; body text should remain comfortable to read on mobile.
• Buttons should have clear labels and adequate touch targets.
• Cards should use subtle borders and/or soft shadows rather than heavy visual effects.
• Use consistent corner radius, spacing, icons and button styles throughout the product.
• Maintain accessible contrast and readable text sizes.
27.9 Responsive & Accessibility Direction
• Student screens must be designed mobile-first.
• Manager, warden and admin dashboards must work well on desktop and remain usable on tablets.
• The interface should remain functional on smaller screens without horizontal scrolling.
• Controls must be easy to tap and visually distinguishable.
• Color must not be the only way to communicate status.
27.10 Brand Feel
• Apni Rasoi should feel like a trustworthy campus food community product.
• The brand personality should be: clean, friendly, simple, fresh, reliable and student-focused.
• The UI should visually support the product's USP: fast feedback, low reading effort and trust/anonymity.
• The design should look polished enough for a real hostel deployment, not like a gaming website or experimental tech demo.
27.11 Direct Instruction for UI Implementation
• Build Apni Rasoi using a predominantly white/light color palette.
• Do not use a dark gaming-style visual theme.
• Prefer clean cards, subtle shadows/borders, rounded controls, readable typography and generous spacing.
• Keep the student feedback flow extremely lightweight and mobile-first.
• Keep dashboards professional and data-focused without making them visually intimidating.
• Maintain the same UI design system across Student, Manager/Provider, Warden/Committee and Platform Admin interfaces.
• The visual design must complement, not alter, the functional requirements and phase-wise feature plan defined earlier in this PRD.
